      How this was measured, and what it can't tell you

      The circle

      The card counts what's within a distance of the spot you pick: 100 to 500 m, 200 m to start (about 650 ft, a 3-minute walk). Distances are as the crow flies, not along the streets. Police reports count at the place LAPD gives them. The card names the spot by the nearest intersection, on the street you picked where it can. The ranks compare the circle with one the same size around each of the City's intersections (where two or more streets meet): “Top 4% of LA intersections” means only 4% have as many or more; 96% have fewer.

      Police reports

      LAPD's crime reports on data.lacity.org from January 2025, when LAPD's new records system is complete, grouped into robbery (including carjacking); assault and other violence (assault, threats, murder, rape and other sex offenses); pickpocketing and purse snatching; weapons; drug offenses; and car break-ins (thefts from a vehicle). A report counts once per group. Only reports in public places count: streets, sidewalks, alleys, parks, parking lots, buses, trains and stations, stores, restaurants and other places open to the public, and homeless encampments. Reports in homes, workplaces, schools, hospitals and hotels aren't counted, nor ones whose place is given only as a vehicle, nor reports of domestic violence. LAPD rounds each report's place to its hundred block or intersection, so the map shows those points, not the exact spot. Where LAPD doesn't know the time, it writes midnight or noon; those reports count, but not in the times of day or in daylight and after dark. The map shades neighborhoods by reports of violence and robbery per km of street.

      High Injury Network

      The High Injury Network for people walking from LADOT's 2024 Vision Zero Safety Study. LADOT scored each street by the crashes there from 2017 to 2021 in which someone was hurt, counting deaths and severe injuries most, and people walking, children and people over 65 more; and by what the street is like: fast or busy traffic, truck routes, schools and bus stops nearby, many people walking, and neighborhoods the City counts as in the most need. The network is the 551 miles that scored highest for people walking: 7.5% of the City's streets, with 72% of the crashes that killed or badly hurt someone walking in those years. The map marks a block as on it when most of the block lies within 20 m of LADOT's line for that street. The card names the streets on it that pass through the circle.

      After dark

      Between sunset and sunrise in Los Angeles on that date, worked out from the date to within a few minutes of the published times. Dusk after sunset and dawn before sunrise count as after dark. A report goes by the time it happened, as the report gives it.

      Streets

      Each line on the map is one stretch of street from one intersection to the next, from the City of Los Angeles street centerlines, named by its hundred block from the house numbers along it. Freeways aren't in the centerlines. The page covers the City of Los Angeles, where LAPD takes the police reports; cities inside or next to it, like Santa Monica, Beverly Hills and Culver City, have their own police.

      Limits

      Police reports show where reports were taken, which depends on where police patrol and on who calls or files online, as much as on where things happen. A street with few reports may just have few people on it; counts aren't adjusted for foot traffic. Drug offenses are reports of having or selling drugs; they don't count overdoses. LAPD's reports run about two weeks behind.
